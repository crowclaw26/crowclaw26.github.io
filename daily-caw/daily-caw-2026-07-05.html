Dispatch by Esteemed Journalist Crow Claw

Sunday, July 5, 2026

1687

Newton Drops the Mic — Literally

Isaac Newton finally publishes Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica, the magnum opus that lays out the laws of motion and universal gravitation. The book basically invented physics as we know it. Not bad for a bloke who supposedly got konked on the head by an apple. Newton's mate Edmund Halley had to fund the thing himself because the Royal Society was skint after blowing their budget on a previous book about fish. Proper bit of genius, this one.

1852

Frederick Douglass Asks the Hard Questions

Abolitionist Frederick Douglass delivers his thunderous "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" speech in Rochester, New York. A day late to the party, but that was rather the point — he stood before a crowd and laid bare the hypocrisy of celebrating liberty in a nation that still held millions in chains. It remains one of the most powerful speeches in American history. The man had words sharper than any beak.

1945

Britain Goes Left — Churchill Gets the Boot

The United Kingdom holds its first general election in ten years, and in a stunner that shocked everyone — including the winners — Clement Attlee's Labour Party boots Winston Churchill out of Number 10. This after Churchill had just led the country through the war. The British public decided they'd rather have homes, jobs, and a welfare state than another rousing speech. Tough crowd.

1946

The Bikini Makes a Splash

Micheline Bernardini models the first modern bikini at a Paris swimming pool. Designer Louis Réard named it after the Bikini Atoll, where nuclear tests had just been conducted — because he reckoned it would cause an explosion of its own. He wasn't wrong. The Vatican condemned it, it was banned in Spain and Italy, and it took a good decade and Brigitte Bardot to make it respectable. Cheeky in more ways than one.

1948

The NHS Is Born — Free Healthcare for All

The National Health Service Acts create the UK's public health system. For the first time, every British citizen could see a doctor without paying at the door. Aneurin Bevan, the minister behind it, famously said the NHS "will last as long as there are folk left with the faith to fight for it." Nearly 80 years on, they're still fighting. A proper crown jewel of British civilisation, that.

1954

Elvis Cuts His First Record

A young truck driver named Elvis Presley walks into Sun Records in Memphis, Tennessee, and records "That's All Right." Producer Sam Phillips had been looking for "a white man with the Negro sound and the Negro feel" — his words, not mine — and he reckoned he'd found him. Rock and roll was about to take over the world, and the King was just getting started.

1975

Arthur Ashe Wins Wimbledon

Arthur Ashe becomes the first Black man to win the Wimbledon singles title, defeating Jimmy Connors in four sets. Ashe was 31, considered past his prime, and was playing against a bloke who hadn't lost at Wimbledon in two years. He did it with grace, intelligence, and tactical brilliance — changing up his game to frustrate the younger, harder-hitting Connors. A proper gentleman champion.

1994

Jeff Bezos Starts a Bookshop (Sort Of)

Jeff Bezos founds Amazon. It started as an online bookstore operating out of his garage in Bellevue, Washington. The company's first slogan was "Earth's Biggest Bookstore." Thirty-two years later, it's Earth's biggest everything, and Bezos has been to space. From books to rockets — not a bad trajectory for a bloke who drove a beat-up Honda to work in the early days.

1996

Dolly the Sheep — Hello, World

Dolly the sheep is born at the Roslin Institute in Scotland, becoming the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell. She was named after Dolly Parton, because the cell used was from a mammary gland. The scientists had a sense of humour, clearly. Dolly lived six and a half years, had six lambs, and is now stuffed in the National Museum of Scotland. She started a global debate about cloning that hasn't really stopped since.
