Friday, 24 July 2026

🗺️ Cartier Plants the Flag — and a 30-Foot Cross (1534)

July 24, 1534

French explorer Jacques Cartier erected a 30-foot cross at Pointe-Penouille and claimed the lands around Gaspé for France. Bold move, considering the locals probably had thoughts on who actually owned the place. Still, it kicked off French Canada, so no hard feelings, Jacques.

Proper cheeky bit of colonialism, that. Shows up, plants a big stick, calls dibs. Can't fault the audacity.

👑 Mary, Queen of Scots, Gets the Boot (1567)

July 24, 1567

Mary, Queen of Scots was forced to abdicate the throne. Her one-year-old son — yes, a literal toddler — became King James VI of Scotland. The wee lad would later go on to become James I of England as well. Not bad for a baby who couldn't even hold a sceptre properly.

Imagine losing your job to a baby who's still in nappies. Mary's reign was a proper shambles by the end. Tough gig, being a Stuart.

🐎 First Wagon Train Over the Rockies (1832)

July 24, 1832

Benjamin Bonneville led the first wagon train across the Rocky Mountains through Wyoming's South Pass. A proper bit of pioneering — opening up the West for all the settlers, dreamers, and folks who just really needed some space from their in-laws.

🏛️ Hiram Bingham Stumbles Upon Machu Picchu (1911)

July 24, 1911

American explorer Hiram Bingham "rediscovered" Machu Picchu, the Lost City of the Incas — though local farmers had known about it all along. Still, he brought it to the world's attention, and now it's one of the New Seven Wonders. Not bad for a geography professor who got a bit lost himself.

"Discovered" is doing a lot of heavy lifting here, mate. Bit like me "discovering" a full biscuit tin you'd already opened. Still — what a spot.

💥 Operation Gomorrah: Hamburg Burns (1943)

July 24, 1943

The RAF began bombing Hamburg in what became Operation Gomorrah — a devastating campaign that lasted until August 3rd. The resulting firestorm killed an estimated 42,600 people. One of the most destructive bombing campaigns of WWII, and a grim reminder of what war really looks like from the ground.

Forty-two thousand souls lost in a firestorm. War's never clean, but this was something else entirely. A dark day to remember, not celebrate.

🎬 High Noon Hits the Silver Screen (1952)

July 24, 1952

American Western film "High Noon" was released, directed by Fred Zinnemann and starring Gary Cooper and Grace Kelly. A tense, real-time Western about a marshal abandoned by his town when trouble comes calling. Won four Oscars and became the film every politician wishes they hadn't used as a metaphor.

🎸 Eye of the Tiger Rules the Charts (1982)

July 24, 1982

The single "Eye of the Tiger" by Survivor began a six-week run at #1 on the US charts. Written for Rocky III, it won a Grammy for Best Rock Performance. You can't hear that opening riff without wanting to punch something — preferably in slow motion up some steps.

Absolute banger. That riff is pure adrenaline in audio form. If this song comes on and you don't feel like you could take on a grizzly bear, check your pulse.

🚴 Lance Armstrong Retires — Then Gets Binned (2005)

July 24, 2005

The 92nd Tour de France ended with no official winner. Lance Armstrong retired after a record seventh consecutive victory — but was disqualified in 2012 for doping. Went from hero to zero faster than a peloton downhill. The most expensive case of "everyone was doing it" in sporting history.

Seven yellow jerseys, all in the bin. From Livestrong to just... strong-arming the truth. What a waste of talent.

🎵 Joni Mitchell's Surprise Return (2022)

July 24, 2022

Singer-songwriter legend Joni Mitchell made a surprise concert appearance at the Newport Folk Festival, joining Brandi Carlile on stage. It was Mitchell's first public performance in 19 years. A proper magical moment for music fans — the folk queen back where she belonged.

🌍 The Planet Is Properly Boiling (2019)

July 24, 2019

Three major studies published in Nature and Nature Geoscience confirmed that global warming is happening faster than at any point in the last 2,000 years — and scientific consensus that humans are responsible hit 99%. The message was clear: we're the ones with the thermostat, and we've got it cranked.

99% consensus. That's not "debate," that's "sorted." The other 1% are the sort of blokes who'd argue the sky's actually chartreuse.

📊 The First Opinion Poll (1824)

July 24, 1824

The Harrisburg Pennsylvanian newspaper published results of the first public opinion poll, showing Andrew Jackson in the lead. And thus, a scourge was born — the 24-hour news cycle's favourite toy and every politician's favourite excuse. "The polls say..."

👔 The Tsar Who Came Back as PM (2001)

July 24, 2001

Simeon Saxe-Coburg-Gotha, who reigned as the last Tsar of Bulgaria as a child, was sworn in as Prime Minister of Bulgaria. He became the first monarch in history to regain political power through a democratic election to a different office. Now that's a career pivot.

Tsar as a nipper, PM as a grown-up. Most of us can't even decide what to have for tea, and this bloke reinvented his entire political career. Fair play, Simeon.
