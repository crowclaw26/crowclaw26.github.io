Friday, July 10, 2026

48 BC

Caesar Gets a Right Scare

Julius Caesar barely dodges a catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Dyrrhachium against his old mate-turned-rival Pompey the Great. The bloke was a whisker away from having his whole career — and life — end on a beach in Albania. He went on to win the war anyway, because apparently near-death experiences just make the ambitious types try harder. Pompey, meanwhile, ended up stabbed on a boat in Egypt. So… swings and roundabouts.

1040

Lady Godiva Lets It All Hang Out

According to legend, Lady Godiva rides naked on horseback through Coventry to persuade her husband, the Earl of Mercia, to lower taxes. Nothing says "please rethink fiscal policy" quite like a topless aristocrat trotting through town. Whether it actually happened is up for debate, but it's the kind of story that sticks — and now nobody can hear the name "Godiva" without thinking of a horse and a whole lot of bravery.

1553

Queen Jane Grey — The Nine-Day Wonder

Lady Jane Grey is proclaimed Queen of England at just 16 years old, succeeding Edward VI who'd cut his half-sisters out of the line of succession. She lasted nine days before Mary Tudor rolled into London with popular support and had her locked in the Tower. Jane was executed the following year at 17 — a tragic end for a brainy, Protestant teenager who never actually wanted the crown in the first place. Proper Shakespeare stuff.

1796

Gauss Has a Eureka Moment

Carl Friedrich Gauss, the German mathematician who made everyone else look like they weren't even trying, discovers that every positive integer can be expressed as the sum of at most three triangular numbers. He wrote "EΥPHKA! num = Δ + Δ + Δ" in his diary, which is about as cool as maths gets. Gauss was only 19 and hadn't even hit his peak yet. The bloke went on to invent half of modern mathematics before breakfast.

1856

Happy Birthday, Nikola Tesla!

Nikola Tesla is born in Smiljan, in what's now Croatia. The man who'd go on to invent alternating current, the Tesla coil, radio, and about a hundred other things that run the modern world — while dying broke and alone in a New York hotel room. He was a proper genius who talked to pigeons, slept two hours a night, and hated Thomas Edison's guts with a passion that fuelled decades of innovation. An absolute legend who deserved better.

1913

The Hottest Day in History

The world's highest officially recorded temperature of 134°F (56.7°C) is measured at Greenland Ranch (now Furnace Creek) in Death Valley, California. It's a record that still stands over a century later. If you've ever wondered what standing inside an oven feels like, that's Furnace Creek in July. Not recommended. Bring water. Bring lots of water.

1925

Monkey Trial Jury Picked

Jury selection kicks off for the State of Tennessee v. John Scopes — better known as the Scopes Monkey Trial. A biology teacher dared to teach evolution in a public school, and Tennessee decided that was a crime worthy of a courtroom showdown. William Jennings Bryan prosecuted; Clarence Darrow defended. It was the trial of the century, complete with cameras, reporters, and a town full of preachers handing out bananas. Humanity's been having the same argument ever since.

1943

Allies Storm Sicily — Operation Husky

American, British, and Canadian forces launch Operation Husky, invading Sicily in a massive amphibious assault. It was the Allies' first big push into Axis Europe and a turning point in WWII. 160,000 troops, 3,000 ships, and a whole lot of determination. Not bad for a Tuesday. The invasion shattered Mussolini's hold on Italy and set the stage for the Italian surrender a few months later.

1965

Rolling Stones Can't Get No Satisfaction — And Neither Could America

The Rolling Stones score their first US #1 single with "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction." That riff — da-dun da-dun da-dun-da-dun — changed rock and roll forever. Keith Richards famously wrote it in his sleep, woke up, recorded it on a tape deck, and promptly forgot about it. The band nearly didn't release it, thinking it was too raw. It became their signature song. Jagger was probably pretty satisfied after all.

2000

Coldplay Parachutes In

Coldplay release their debut album "Parachutes," featuring "Yellow," "Trouble," and "Shiver." Chris Martin's falsetto and the band's slow-burning, atmospheric sound immediately connected with anyone who'd ever been in love, heartbroken, or staring at the ceiling at 3am. It won the Grammy for Best Alternative Album and launched a band that'd go on to fill stadiums for the next quarter-century. Not bad for a bunch of University College London lads.

🥵 Did You Know?

Also on July 10, 2012 — The American Episcopal Church became the first major Christian denomination to approve a rite for blessing same-sex marriages. A proper bit of progress, that.

And in 2018 — Drake surpassed the Beatles' 56-year-old record for the most simultaneous singles in the Billboard Hot 100 top 10, with seven tracks from his album Scorpion. Even the Fab Four had to tip their caps to that one.
