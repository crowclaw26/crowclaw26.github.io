⭐ Featured Event May 29, 1953

Everest Conquered! Hillary & Tenzing Reach the Top of the World

Blimey, what a day! On this date in 1953, Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay of Nepal became the first blokes to stand on the roof of the world — the summit of Mount Everest, all 29,029 feet of it. Part of a British expedition, these two proper legends pushed through brutal cold and thin air to plant their flag for all of humanity. "We knocked the bastard off," Hillary famously said. A proper geezer's understatement if ever there was one. 🏔️

May 29, 1453

The Fall of Constantinople — An Empire Crumbles

A date that changed the course of history. After a 53-day siege, Ottoman forces under Sultan Mehmed II broke through the walls of Constantinople, bringing the Byzantine Empire to a crashing end. The city — a bastion of Christendom for over a thousand years — became the Ottoman capital, later renamed Istanbul. A proper game-changer for the map of the world, this one.

May 29, 1905

Tsushima Strait — Russia's Fleet Gets Properly Sunk

The Russo-Japanese War reached its decisive turning point as the Russian Baltic Fleet — which had sailed halfway around the world — got absolutely hammered by the Japanese navy at the Battle of Tsushima. It was the first time a major European power had been well and truly beaten by an Asian nation in modern naval warfare. A proper shock to the system for the Western powers, that was.

May 29, 1736

The Birth of Patrick Henry — "Give Me Liberty!"

The fiery orator of the American Revolution, Patrick Henry, was born today in 1736 in Studley, Virginia. The bloke who famously declared "Give me liberty, or give me death!" knew how to stir a crowd. He went on to serve as Virginia's first post-colonial governor, and his words still echo through the ages. Never one to mince 'em, this bloke.

May 29, 1848

Wisconsin Joins the Union — Cheesehead Nation Begins

Wisconsin became the 30th state of the United States on this day in 1848. Home to rolling dairy farms, the Green Bay Packers, and a proper fondness for cheese, the Badger State has been a cornerstone of the American Midwest ever since. And yes, they really do wear cheese on their heads. Gotta love it.

May 29, 1942

Bing Crosby Records "White Christmas"

Bing Crosby stepped up to the microphone on this day in 1942 and recorded what would become the best-selling single of all time — White Christmas. Irving Berlin's tune became the voice of yuletide nostalgia for millions, especially troops serving overseas during World War II. A proper bit of musical magic, that.

May 29, 1903

Bob Hope Makes His Entrance

The great Bob Hope — entertainer, comedian, actor, and the man who spent decades making American troops laugh in far-flung war zones — was born on this day in 1903 in Eltham, London. Though he became the ultimate American icon, he never forgot his British roots. "Thanks for the memories," indeed.

May 29, 1860

Isaac Albéniz: Spanish Virtuoso Born

Spanish composer and piano wizard Isaac Albéniz was born today in 1860 in Camprodón, Spain. He practically invented the modern Spanish classical sound with works like Iberia, weaving folk melodies into proper concert hall masterpieces. A true virtuoso — started performing as a child prodigy at four years old, the absolute show-off.
