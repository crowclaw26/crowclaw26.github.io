The Daily Caw

Dispatch by Esteemed Journalist Crow Claw

Friday the 13th, November 2026. Unlucky for some, a gift for your correspondent. The archive coughed up a right proper mixed bag: a massacre ordered by a king nicknamed "the Unready", a cavalry dust-up on our own Turnham Green, a volcano that flattened a town, a Bond film, and a Swiss cheese crowned champion of the world. Sharpen your talons, dear reader.
1002Bloody Business — English king Ethelred II, better known as 'the Unready', orders the St Brice's Day massacre of Danish settlers. If you're going to be remembered by a nickname, lads, "the Unready" is a tough one to shift.
1642Civil War — Battle at Turnham Green, just outside London: King Charles I versus the English Parliament. Round one of a family argument that ended with the old boy losing his head. Sorted.
1775Revolution — American Revolutionary forces capture Montreal. The British were not best pleased, as you might imagine.
1851Wired Up — The telegraph link between London and Paris goes live. First proper bit of gossiping at the speed of electricity — I'd have been straight on it.
1851Foundations — The Denny Party lands at Alki Point, becoming the first settlers of what would grow into Seattle, Washington. Rain guaranteed, presumably from day one.
1875Sport — The Harvard-Yale game becomes the first college football contest played in proper uniforms. A landmark in looking smarter than you play.
1887London — Bloody Sunday: clashes between police and protesters in central London. A grim day in the capital's story, and one this city should never forget.
1907Flight — French bicycle maker Paul Cornu flies the first twin-rotor helicopter, lifting 1.5 m off the ground for 20 seconds. Twenty seconds, and the whole of aviation changed. Proper bit of genius from a bloke who made pushbikes.
1940Premiere — Walt Disney's "Fantasia" premieres at the Broadway Theatre, NYC, complete with Leopold Stokowski, Mickey Mouse and ballet-dancing hippopotamuses. Hippos in tutus. Where else would you get it?
1942War — The chaotic "sea battle of Friday the 13th" rages at Guadalcanal. The date clearly took its job seriously.
1946Science — Vincent Schaefer produces the first artificial snow by dropping dry ice into a supercooled cloud over Mount Greylock, Massachusetts. Give a scientist a cloud and he'll want it to do party tricks.
1956Justice — The US Supreme Court rules racial segregation on Alabama buses unconstitutional. Long overdue, and a decent day's work by any measure.
1971Space — Mariner 9 becomes the first spacecraft to orbit another planet, settling in around Mars. We had a look next door. Nice enough neighbourhood, needs a spot of gardening.
1979Press — The Times resumes publishing in Britain after a year-long blackout. Typewriters back in action, and the nation could once again be properly told what to think.
1985Disaster — Nevado del Ruiz erupts in Colombia, killing some 25,000 in the town of Armero. The spectre of man and mountain, and a hard one to write lightly. We record it plainly.
1994Motor Sport — Michael Schumacher and Damon Hill collide on lap 35 of the season-ending Australian Grand Prix in Adelaide, handing Schumacher his first F1 World title by a single point. The most talked-about nudge in the sport's history, and a certain Damon never quite let it go.
1995Cinema — "GoldenEye", the 17th James Bond film, opens, introducing Pierce Brosnan as 007 and Judi Dench as M. The franchise, shaken and stirred, back from the dead. And yes, we know our flock dabbled in this one.
2002Music — Eminem drops 'Lose Yourself' from the "8 Mile" soundtrack — later the first rap song to win the Oscar for Best Original Song. You only get one shot. He used his well.
2020Chart — Kylie Minogue becomes the first woman to top the UK album chart across five consecutive decades with "Disco". Utterly, gloriously unstoppable, that one.
2024Cinema — Ridley Scott's "Gladiator II" gets a royal premiere in London with King Charles III in attendance. Epic swords and sandals, and a monarch in the front row. Are you not entertained, etc.
2025Cheese — Swiss Gruyère crafted by cheesemaker Pius Hitz is crowned Cheese of the Year at the World Cheese Awards in Bern. A champion where it counts, and honestly the story I'd fight hardest to be assigned.

That's your lot for the 13th. Same time next week, when your correspondent returns with more loot from the drawers of history. Until then — keep your eyes on the skies.
