Friday, 28 August 2026

Blimey, what a date. 28 August has seen crusades, charters, booed folk legends, and the birth of Pepsi. Let's have a proper rummage through the archives, shall we?

🏛️ Historical Events

1189 — Third Crusade

Siege of Acre Begins

Crusaders under Guy of Lusignan kick off the Siege of Acre, one of the most epic medieval stand-offs you'll ever read about. Took 'em nearly two years to wrap it up, but this was the first stone in that bloody pile.

1207 — King John's Charter

Liverpool Gets Its Papers

King John of England — yeah, the bad King John, the Robin Hood one — grants the small town of Liverpool a charter to elect its own mayor and aldermen. Little did he know it'd one day be famous for The Beatles, two cathedrals, and lads who can't handle their ale. Sorted, lad.

1609

Henry Hudson Sails Into Delaware Bay

English explorer Henry Hudson becomes the first European to sail into Delaware Bay, calling it "South Bay". He was on the payroll of the Dutch East India Company at the time, proper mercenary exploration work. He'd go on to find that river in New York that now bears his name.

1830

Horse Beats A Train — Honest

The first American-built locomotive, "Tom Thumb", races a horse-drawn carriage from Baltimore to Ellicott Mills. And the horse won. The train had mechanical problems, so for one glorious day, good old-fashioned horsepower showed steam power who was boss.

1845

Scientific American Hits the Shelves

Scientific American magazine publishes its very first issue. 181 years later, it's still going — the oldest continuously published monthly magazine in the US. Not bad for a penny paper started by a bloke named Rufus Porter.

1898

Pepsi Is Born

Caleb Bradham renames his carbonated soft drink "Pepsi-Cola". Originally called "Brad's Drink", the name comes from "pepsin" and "cola". A fizzing success story was born.

1913 — Peace Palace Opens

A Palace For Peace in The Hague

Queen Wilhelmina of the Netherlands opens the Peace Palace in The Hague. Built with cash from Andrew Carnegie, it houses the Permanent Court of Arbitration. Bit of a contrast with what happened across Europe the very next year, but the building's still doing its job.

1914 — World War I

Battle of Heligoland Bight

The British fleet decisively defeats the Germans in the North Sea. Nearly 800 dead, over 200 wounded. This was the first major naval battle of WWI, and the Royal Navy showed they weren't mucking about.

🎵 Music & Culture

1965

Dylan Goes Electric, Gets Booed

Bob Dylan takes the stage at Forest Hills Tennis Stadium in Queens, New York, plugs in his electric guitar, and the folk purists absolutely lose it. They booed him off. But history's had the last laugh — electric Dylan is now legendary. Take that, acoustic snobs.

1970

Jackson 5 Drop "I'll Be There"

"I'll Be There" by The Jackson 5 is released. It went on to be Billboard's Song of the Year in 1970. Young Michael Jackson, already a star, delivering a vocal that still gives you goosebumps.

1973

Marvin Gaye's "Let's Get It On"

Tamla/Motown releases Marvin Gaye's 13th studio album "Let's Get It On". A masterpiece of soul and sensuality. Also on this day: "Smoke on the Water" by Deep Purple goes gold. Riff of the century, that one.

2009

Oasis Breaks Up — Noël Leaves the Band

Noel Gallagher quits Oasis, citing an "inability to be with his brother Liam any longer". Shocker to absolutely nobody who'd watched them for 15 years. The Gallagher brothers' legendary feud finally killed one of Britain's greatest bands. Still, we'll always have "Wonderwall".

🎬 Film & Television

1956

Venice Film Festival — No Prize Awarded

The 17th Venice Film Festival opens, but here's the kicker — no Golden Lion was awarded that year. Bit of a damp squib for the filmmakers who turned up.

1986

Tina Turner Gets Her Star

American rock legend Tina Turner receives a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. Simply the best, as she'd say. Well deserved.

⚽ Sport

1921

Babe Ruth's Extra-Base Streak

Babe Ruth starts a streak of hitting an extra-base hit in nine consecutive games. The Sultan of Swat was basically a cheat code in human form.

1972 — Munich Olympics

Olga Korbut Becomes a Star

Soviet gymnast Olga Korbut becomes a media darling at the Munich Olympics, winning gold in the team all-around plus two individual golds and a silver. The 17-year-old charmed the world with her daring routines and that famous smile.

🎂 Born On This Day

Johann Wolfgang von Goethe (1749–1832)

German literary giant, philosopher, and all-round polymath. Wrote "Faust", "The Sorrows of Young Werther", and basically shaped German literature for centuries. Absolute legend.

Sheridan Le Fanu (1814–1873)

Irish writer of Gothic horror and mystery. Wrote "The House by the Churchyard" and "Carmilla" — the latter being a vampire story that predates Dracula by a good 26 years. Underappreciated genius.

Edward Burne-Jones (1833–1898)

English Pre-Raphaelite painter and designer. Stained glass, tapestries, paintings — the bloke did it all. His work still graces cathedrals across Britain.
